The library

What exactly does Callista cover?

The WIFU Bibliothek — 459 publications across 515 language editions: Praxisleitfäden, Praxisartikel, WIFU_kompakt, Studien, the WIFU-Schriftenreihe and books, published between 2007 and 2026. Callista holds the full text of each, split into 8,685 sections, plus 1,313 figures.

Is it up to date?

The corpus is refreshed from wifu.de periodically rather than continuously. A publication that appeared this week may not be in yet. The figures on the overview page carry the date they were counted.

Most of it is in German. I don’t read German.

That is much of the point. Claude reads the German for you: ask in English, and it will search the German material and answer in English, quoting and translating as it goes. Callista indexes German text with German stemming and English text with English stemming, so neither language is searched approximately.

What you lose is nuance in translation. For anything you intend to cite, follow the link and read the original.

Can I get the PDFs?

Not through Callista. It returns the text, the metadata and a link to the publication’s page on wifu.de, where WIFU distributes the PDF on their own terms.

Why do some publications have richer summaries than others?

Because a semantic enrichment pass — abstracts, keywords, topic classification, figure descriptions — is still running across the corpus. Where it has landed you get a fuller summary; where it has not, you get the publisher’s own excerpt, which is a perfectly good answer. Search works across everything either way. Coverage of the enrichment layer is currently low and growing.

A work appears once but has two editions. Why?

A German original and its English translation are two editions of one work. Results are grouped at the level of the work, with its editions listed underneath, so a bilingual publication does not take two slots in your results just for existing twice.

Access

Who can use it?

People who have been invited. There is no domain rule and no self-service sign-up — access is granted one email address at a time, by hand.

I was invited but it says I am not on the list.

Almost always the address. Access is tied to the exact address the invitation went to; a personal address, an alias, or a different institutional address will be turned away. Tell us which address you would prefer and we can move it.

Can I share my access?

Please don’t. Sign-in is tied to your mailbox, and usage is attributed to you. If a colleague needs access, ask for them — it takes a moment.

How do I stop using it?

Remove the connector in Claude’s settings, and tell us if you would like the grant withdrawn as well. Withdrawal takes effect on the next request, not whenever a token happens to expire.

Privacy and limits

What do you log?

Which tool was called, the query text, how many results came back, how long it took, and which account made the call. Nothing from your wider conversation with Claude reaches Callista — it only ever sees the search or lookup Claude sends it.

The queries that return nothing are read as a to-do list: they show what people expect the library to answer and it currently does not.

Is there a rate limit?

Forty tool calls an hour per account. It is a ceiling against runaway loops, not a quota — ordinary use will not reach it. Failed calls are not counted against you.

Can Callista write anything, or change the library?

No. It has read-only access to the corpus and no ability to modify it.

Provenance

Is this a WIFU product?

No. The publications are WIFU’s and remain theirs. Callista is operated by CeFEO at Jönköping International Business School as a way of reading that literature inside Claude. Anything Callista returns should be attributed to the WIFU publication it came from, not to Callista.

Can I cite Callista?

Cite the underlying publication. Callista gives you the title, authors, year and a link precisely so that you can. If you want to describe how you found the material, naming the tool in a methods note is fine.

Claude summarised something incorrectly.

It can. Callista returns source text faithfully, but the summary on top of it is generated. For anything consequential, open the linked publication and check. Report anything that looks systematically wrong — that is usually a retrieval problem worth fixing.
